Chenyun Dai

Tenure-Track Associate Professor · Ph.D. Supervisor · Biomedical Engineering at Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Office · Bldg. 3–209

Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Shanghai, China

I am a tenure-track Associate Professor and Ph.D. supervisor at the School of Biomedical Engineering, Shanghai Jiao Tong University. I joined the faculty in 2023.

My research seeks to restore and augment human motor function by combining medical electronics, neural and motor biosensing, artificial intelligence, and robotics. We develop complete systems that connect physiological signal acquisition, human-intent decoding, intelligent control, and assistive or stimulation-based execution.

Our current programs span implantable artificial spinal cords and neural systems for people with paralysis, intelligent human–machine interaction for neural prostheses and exoskeletons, and brain–computer interfaces for embodied robots that learn human movement from motion capture, inertial sensing, and surface electromyography.

Research at a glance

01 · RESTORE
Artificial neural systems

Implantable artificial spinal cords, artificial neural systems, and peripheral rehabilitation technologies for people with paralysis.

02 · INTERACT
Intelligent human–machine interfaces

Biosignal-driven intent recognition and embedded intelligent hardware for prostheses, exoskeletons, and interactive systems.

03 · EMBODY
Brain–computer interfaces and embodied intelligence

Multimodal human-motion learning that enables embodied robots to acquire and imitate human behavioral capabilities.

Translation and impact

Our group works at the interface of discovery, engineering, and deployment. Multiple prosthetic technologies have been translated into commercial products and featured in a follow-up report by a Boston television station. Our human–machine interaction research has also supported the development of an interaction module for Huawei Watch through funding from Huawei Central Research Institute.

Over the past three years, I have published more than 50 papers and led two National Natural Science Foundation of China projects and two projects at the provincial or ministerial level. I have been recognized by the Shanghai Overseas High-Level Talent Program and the Shanghai Pujiang Talent Program.

Before joining Shanghai Jiao Tong University, I worked at the Shanghai Municipal Development and Reform Commission, where I advanced policy reforms for industry–academia–research collaboration and contributed the chapter on emerging technology industries to Shanghai’s 14th Five-Year Plan.
