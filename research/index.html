research

From neural and motor signals to intelligent assistance, stimulation, and embodied action.

We develop integrated bioelectronic and robotic systems that sense human physiological states, decode movement intention, and restore or augment motor function. Across three research programs, a common engineering pipeline links biosignal acquisition to clinically relevant stimulation, assistance, or embodied action.

Neural and motor biosensing  →  Intent decoding  →  Intelligent control  →  Stimulation or assistive action

01 · Neural function restoration and rehabilitation

Artificial spinal cords and closed-loop neural systems

We develop implantable artificial spinal cords and closed-loop neural systems for people with paralysis, alongside peripheral technologies for motor assistance and rehabilitation. These systems seek to bridge disrupted neural pathways by converting residual neural and motor signals into coordinated stimulation and movement.

System focus:

  • implantable neural interfaces and application-specific medical electronics;
  • closed-loop sensing, decoding, and stimulation;
  • peripheral assistive and rehabilitation systems;
  • clinically relevant evaluation of motor recovery.

02 · Intelligent human–machine interaction

From biosignals to intuitive control

We create human–machine interfaces that decode movement intention from neural and motor biosignals. Our work integrates electronic hardware, adaptive artificial intelligence, and embedded or desktop software into end-to-end systems for reliable, low-latency control.

Application focus:

  • neural prostheses and wearable exoskeletons;
  • accessible computing, including virtual mouse interfaces;
  • drone control and movement-based interactive systems;
  • robust, personalized decoding across users, sessions, and environments.

03 · Brain–computer interfaces and embodied intelligence

Learning human behavior for embodied robots

We study how embodied robots can acquire transferable motor skills from human demonstrations. Motion capture, inertial measurement units (IMUs), and surface electromyography (sEMG) provide complementary information about body kinematics, dynamics, and neuromuscular intention.

Research focus:

  • synchronized acquisition of human movement and neuromuscular data;
  • multimodal representations across motion capture, IMUs, and sEMG;
  • imitation learning and intention-aware learning from human demonstrations;
  • adaptation and transfer of learned skills to embodied robotic platforms.

Translational engineering

Hardware

Medical electronics, neural and wearable sensors, and integrated prototypes.

Algorithms

Multimodal representation learning, intent recognition, and intelligent control.

Deployment

Embedded software, clinical validation, and product-oriented translation.

Our priority is not isolated algorithmic performance, but integrated systems that remain reliable beyond laboratory conditions. Several technologies have reached commercial products.
